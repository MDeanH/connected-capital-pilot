How it works

From the sponsor's documents to a defensible verdict.

Upload an offering memorandum and a pro forma. The platform reads both, shows its receipts, runs the deal through a hard-kill rulebook under live macro conditions, and records a decision you can replay and defend — with a human in control at every step.

The flow

One deal, five minutes, no blank forms.

1

Drop in the documents

The offering memorandum (PDF) and the pro forma (Excel). The workbook is parsed in your browser — only text leaves the machine.

2

Extraction with receipts

AI pulls ~30 screening fields — costs, contingency, leverage, NOI, rents, entitlement, sponsor terms. Every value carries a confidence score and a citation back to the document and page. It never guesses: a fact the documents don't state comes back empty and is flagged for you.

3

The documents get cross-examined

The OM and the pro forma should tell the same story. Every disagreement — NOI, unit count, timeline, rents, capital structure — is listed with a severity tag. A deal's own paperwork contradicting itself is a diligence signal no spreadsheet catches.

4

You verify, then it scores

Review what was extracted, correct anything, answer what the documents couldn't. Nothing is scored until you confirm.

5

Gate 1: ten hard kills, eight watch flags

Character, entitlement, leverage, development spread, contingency, alignment, track record, exit dependence, construction protection, and a stress test. Any single hard kill fails the deal — strengths don't average away a fatal flaw. Flags accumulate into conditional approval. Every check shows its margin to the line, and thin passes are marked fragile.

PASSCONDITIONAL FAILREVIEW — missing data never passes
6

The economy moves the bar

Every week, eight economic signals — recession risk, inflation, growth, credit spreads, the labor market — vote on the market regime, which sets a Green / Yellow / Red posture. Posture tightens or loosens the thresholds automatically: the same deal can clear the gate in a green week and fail it in a red one. Macro moves the bar; it never kills a deal by itself.

7

Judgment, on the record

See something the model can't? Adjust any threshold for this deal — with a required written reason. The rulebook's original verdict is kept alongside yours, so every override is visible, attributed, and reversible. Manual control is never silent.

8

The market reality check

The deal's returns stand next to what the S&P 500 actually returned over one, three and five years — and the loan rate the model assumes is checked against prevailing rates (SOFR plus a typical spread). Returns that lean on financing nobody offers get called out.

9

Your rulebook, not ours

Every threshold, band, weight and stress parameter is editable configuration — there isn't a single hard-coded number in the engine. Changes are saved as new versions; past decisions always know which version judged them.

10

Back-test your own history

Load past deals with their realized outcomes and see how today's rulebook would have called each one — right calls, missed winners, and whether the book ran above or below par. The gate earns trust by being scored against reality.

The rules the code enforces

Not policies in a document — invariants in the build.

Zero hardcoded thresholds

Every tunable lives in versioned config. If a threshold were a literal in the codebase, it would be a bug.

Every decision replayable

Recorded with its inputs, config version, macro posture and adjustments — any past verdict can be reproduced exactly.

Missing data never passes

Extraction returns nothing rather than guessing, and an unanswered field forces human review — silence can't slip through.

Macro moves the bar, never kills

Conditions make the gate stricter or looser; only the deal's own facts can fail it.

See it on a real deal

The app ships with a recorded extraction of a real example deal — strong headline returns that fail three hard kills, with six contradictions between its own documents.
